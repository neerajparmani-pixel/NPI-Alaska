# Operations 3.2.0 recordings and narration

Jenny (Dioco), English female synthetic narration, using Piper en_GB-jenny_dioco-medium. Dataset and terms: https://github.com/dioco-group/jenny-tts-dataset. Source README permits commercial use and distribution of generated clips; attribution is included. Runtime: https://github.com/OHF-Voice/piper1-gpl (GPL-3.0). Model conversion: https://github.com/k2-fsa/sherpa-onnx/releases/tag/tts-models. Model card included. No model weights are distributed. Australian online endpoint could not be reached; these videos use an English (Great Britain) voice and do not claim an Australian accent.


All three videos record the running local frontend with an intercepted fictional backend. GPS/camera are simulated; the ruler drawing is a labelled training illustration. A displayed 105-minute shift is a fixture, not filming duration. Actual saved test upload bytes are shown in all three role galleries. Narration is normalised to -16 LUFS with chapter headings, burned-in captions and separate VTT captions. No music or live personal records are used. Source: scripts/record-v32-training.mjs and scripts/render-v32-training.py in the NPI-Alaska repository; source snapshots are included. Reproduction needs the repository dependencies, Piper model referenced above, piper-tts, Chromium and FFmpeg. Model weights and test-server interception are not part of the deployed frontend. Voice clarity has technical checks and known-script comparison, not independent human accent certification.

The training-camera.mjs helper generates the labelled ruler camera feed with Chromium and FFmpeg. Set NPI_VOICE_MODEL to your downloaded Piper model path. Generated WAV files can be reused by the renderer without loading Piper again.

New recordings cover email-bound signup, verification, approvals and complete reports. Email, camera, location, Auth and database responses are intercepted fictional fixtures; actual local report and evidence files are generated by the app. No messages were sent to real people.
